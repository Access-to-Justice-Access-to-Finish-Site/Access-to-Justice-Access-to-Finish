Frequently Asked Questions

The project, in plain language.

Short answers about the project’s purpose, evidence standard, privacy boundaries, and current direction.

Start here: I am no longer seeking readmission to Western Law. Earlier return requests remain in the archive as historical context.
What is This Could Have Been You?

It is an independent storytelling and advocacy project documenting my experience with Western Law and overlapping academic, administrative, human-rights, and legal processes.

What is the project asking for now?

The project seeks public understanding and accountability, responsible engagement with the record, better institutional practices, and room to build a future beyond Western Law. It is not a readmission campaign.

Why do older pages discuss returning or finishing the JD?

Those requests were part of the documented history in 2025–2026. Evidence records describe them in the past tense because changing the historical record would make the chronology less accurate.

Is the public being asked to decide legal or human-rights claims?

No. The site distinguishes personal experience, source records, allegations, institutional positions, and findings. A filing is evidence that a claim was made, not proof that it succeeded.

What evidence is published?

The current site publishes an index and contextual summaries. Underlying source files, recordings, and transcripts remain withheld unless they can be verified, lawfully shared, contextualized, and safely redacted.

Why are some records withheld?

Transparency does not require publishing private medical information, personal contact details, privileged or confidential material, or irrelevant information about third parties. The purpose is verification, not exposure.

How can people help?

Read carefully, share responsibly, make relevant introductions, and advocate for better institutional systems. Harassment, threats, doxxing, and contacting named people on my behalf are not requested.

Where should journalists start?

Begin with the press page, then review the story, timeline, evidence archive, and publication standards before requesting source materials or comment.
