The System

When every route points somewhere else.

A person can have access to many procedures and still lack access to a timely, practical remedy.

The problem documented here is not the absence of institutions, policies, or processes. It is the gap between having somewhere to file, complain, appeal, or seek review—and obtaining an outcome that can be used in real life.

Back to the personThe practical harm and uncertainty remain.

Why the loop matters

Academic and professional time is perishable. Terms begin, registration windows close, and opportunities depend on decisions arriving while they can still make a difference. A process that may eventually address a past wrong does not necessarily prevent present harm.

Access becomes a maze when the existence of multiple routes obscures the absence of a meaningful remedy.

What accountability can look like

Accountability can include clear authority, timely decisions, trauma-informed communication, usable accommodations, preservation of records, independent review, and honest acknowledgement when processes have compounded harm.

This project documents how those systems interacted in one person’s experience. It invites readers to consider how they could work differently.
