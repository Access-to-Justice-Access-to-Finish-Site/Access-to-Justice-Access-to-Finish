Why this meeting matters

The recording documents concerns raised through an internal human-rights channel, including accounts concerning racial discrimination, academic accommodations, trauma, grading, institutional treatment and professional consequences. It also records the responses, procedural explanations and anti-reprisal guidance provided during the meeting.

The recording establishes what participants said during the meeting. It does not convert disputed allegations into adjudicated findings.

Evidence integrity

Recording Authenticity & Verification

Recording authenticity

This recording documents a meeting I personally attended with university officials. The conversation involved identifiable participants who can independently corroborate the meeting and the statements made.

Supporting documentation

The recording forms part of a broader collection of contemporaneous evidence, including university correspondence, meeting records, transcripts and related documentation. These materials provide context and allow readers to independently assess the chronology and statements.

Recording transparency

The original source recording is preserved separately from the publicly released listening copies. The two published copies are edited versions of the meeting with the same content at different playback speeds. Portions concerning students, sensitive third-party experiences and material not necessary to understand the institutional process have been removed. Further edits, excerpts or redactions made for privacy or publication purposes will be identified as they are verified and documented.

Independent forensic authentication

On October 15, 2026, I contacted Audio Forensics Canada to request information about independent forensic authentication, including the possibility of obtaining a formal expert report. The inquiry is pending. No independent forensic examination or authentication finding is being claimed.

Meeting scope

What the discussion addressed

Concerns raised

Racial discrimination, academic treatment, accommodation barriers, trauma-related impacts, grading concerns and the effect on legal education and professional plans.

Institutional response

Discussion of the Human Rights Office process, how concerns could be framed or documented, available procedural routes and guidance about reporting possible reprisal.
