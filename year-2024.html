Requested solutions, actual support and unresolved barriers

Dated timeline ↗

The practical requests: temporary Zoom access for classes when safety, health, family circumstances or travel costs interfered; a neutral decision-maker independent of the faculty members named in complaints; alternative communication channels; and a workable completion plan without repeated exposure to reported campus or classroom concerns. These requests did not seek an automatic exemption from all academic standards.

16 August 2024 — remote-access discussion: In the recorded meeting with Acting Dean Botterell, the student asked about flexibility and remote participation. The account of the recording at approximately 01:11:55–01:12:55 describes Botterell acknowledging some professors could meet or teach remotely but saying the broader option would not work because of the in-person model and available resources. The distinction between individual course Zoom links and remote completion of the entire JD matters. The original recording should be checked before publishing a verbatim cost quotation.

10 September 2024 — a solution that worked: After the student disclosed serious family, financial and access-related circumstances, Assistant Dean (EDI) Sunil Gurmukh requested compassionate Zoom access for a class. The course professor provided the link that afternoon; Associate Dean Sherrin was copied. This is documented support worth acknowledging, and concrete evidence that temporary remote participation could be arranged in at least one course.

Commuting and financial effects: The student had reported difficulty affording travel between Milton and London, delayed student funding and instability in family support. Repeated campus attendance, meetings, documentation and requests for help added travel and time burdens, compounded by uncertainty over academic status. Specific dollar totals are not published without receipts or another verified calculation.

Safety and comparison: The student also reported harassment in academic spaces, including the library, and concerns about interacting with professors named in her complaints while those concerns remained unresolved. According to separate anonymized gender-based-violence comparator material, another student described receiving Zoom access to multiple classes and altered attendance arrangements because campus attendance felt unsafe. That is evidence of a different accommodation practice, not proof that the two students’ circumstances or legal entitlements were identical.

Policies and question: The relevant issues concern individualized consideration, accessibility and the availability of alternative participation—not a claim that all law courses could automatically be moved online. What assistance was considered, what could be implemented course by course, and whether the remaining obstacles were referred to a neutral decision-maker?

Private evidence: 16 August 2024 meeting summary, 01:11:55–01:12:55; 10 September 2024 compassionate Zoom email exchange (instructor name and meeting details withheld); Fall 2024 WHRO recording; anonymized GBV comparator record; contemporaneous disclosure of commuting hardship. Publication: role-based references for supportive staff and unnamed students.
