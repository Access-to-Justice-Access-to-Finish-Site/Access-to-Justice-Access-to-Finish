What Western knew, what its policies required, and what followed

Dated emails ↓

This is a linked analysis of institutional notice and decision-making, not a finding that a policy was breached. Each step distinguishes the written policy, the documented action, and the unresolved procedural question. Original private correspondence is identified by date and subject rather than reproduced.

1. Human Rights Office: complaint, notice and threatened withdrawal

Earlier notice: On 31 January, a written complaint was delivered to the Human Rights Office. On 4 March, Aisha Raja confirmed that the office would continue working on it. On 9 March, the student forwarded Christopher Sherrin’s 7 March letter and attachment to Raja. The letter warned that withdrawal would follow if no petition was received by 11 April. On 18 March, Raja asked whether a formal complaint had been filed with AVP-HR.

Relevant policy / expected process

Western’s MAPP 1.35 Procedures (effective 6 February 2025), sections II.1.4 and VII.1–1.3, assign the Human Rights Office an advisory and support role, require confidential consultation, and identify alternate resolution and formal investigation pathways. Section V provides a distinct route for alleged reprisal or retaliation. The Human Rights Office was not itself the academic decision-maker.

Documented response / question

Raja referred the student for GBSV-related academic support on 6 March and later followed up on the AVP-HR complaint. The record establishes that the Human Rights Director received the withdrawal correspondence before the 11 April deadline. The question is how the reported reprisal and academic impact were assessed, and whether appropriate support or referrals followed. Receipt does not by itself establish that Raja could cancel an academic decision.

Connection forward: Formal AVP-HR complaint → Sinel’s April review. Sources: 31 January complaint email; 4, 6, 9 and 18 March messages; Sherrin letter of 7 March.

2. AVP-HR: formal complaint, reprisal concerns and alternative resolution

What was submitted: The student filed a formal complaint with Jane O’Brien on 25 March. The AVP-HR office responded on 31 March. After an 2 April meeting, Sunil Gurmukh suggested a request for an alternative path to graduation on 3 April. The student made that request to Larissa Bartlett on 13 April.

Relevant policy / expected process

MAPP 1.35 Procedures, VII.3.1–3.4, govern written complaints, screening and communication of next steps (within 30 days). Section VII.2 governs alternate resolution, including discretion to decline it and reasons if requested. Crucially, section V states that an alleged reprisal or retaliation is grounds for a complaint proceeding directly to fact-finding. The MAPP 1.35 Policy, III.10, permits internal action to be suspended while overlapping HRTO proceedings continue; it does not establish that suspension is mandatory in every case.

Documented response / question

On 15 April, Bartlett stated that the complaint concerning Botterell and Sherrin was held in abeyance because of the HRTO proceeding, that the Gagan complaint would not proceed, and that alternate resolution could not proceed while the complaint was held in abeyance. She directed academic-progression questions to Western Law. The student responded the same day that the Human Rights Director had been told about the withdrawal letter and reprisal concerns, and requested interdepartmental guidance. The distinct issue is whether the alleged reprisal was separately assessed under section V, and what coordination or referral was provided; the correspondence alone does not resolve those questions.

Connection backward: Raja already had the March 7 letter. Connection forward: Sinel’s review continued while HR declined the requested resolution. Sources: 25 and 31 March; 2, 3, 13 and 15 April emails.

3. Zoë Sinel: an alternative-resolution request became a continuing-enrolment decision

What was requested: The 11 April email asked for an alternative way to complete the JD. On 14 April, Sinel acknowledged that this message did not appear to be a petition. On 15 April, she offered a written or recorded oral petition; the student chose a Zoom meeting. After the 16 April meeting, the student sought more time and provided complaint materials, recordings and transcripts on 23 April. No separate formal written petition was filed. The subsequent meeting and materials were treated as an oral petition and supporting submissions.

Relevant policy / expected process

Sherrin’s 7 March letter expressly invoked Western Law’s Continuing Enrolment policy: multiple final F grades, review of C− or lower grades, and an opportunity to explain why the student should remain enrolled before a final decision. Sherrin stated there was no assessment work to re-read and designated Sinel after Botterell recused himself. Separately, MAPP 1.35 Policy, III.7, provides for individual consideration when applying academic requirements. These provisions do not themselves confer a degree or waive outstanding credits.

Documented response / question

On 17 April, the student wrote that she had been surprised during the meeting to learn the complaint context had not already been shared with Sinel. This is evidence of what the student reported, not independent proof of what every office transmitted. On 1 May, Sinel clarified that she was deciding continued enrolment, not degree conferral. Her 2 May decision lists the complaint, WHRO audio and other materials received, says she considered them for continued enrolment but did not determine the human-rights allegations, and refuses permission to remain enrolled. The central question is whether the policy-based review and individualized consideration adequately addressed the documented complaint-related barriers and support history.

Connection backward: Raja’s notice and the complaint held in abeyance. Connection forward: May 2 withdrawal outcome and subsequent academic remedies. Sources: Sherrin letter (7 March); Sinel emails (11 April–2 May); Sinel’s four-page decision (2 May).

4. The resulting institutional gap

What the documents show: Human Rights had received the threatened-withdrawal letter; AVP-HR declined to pursue alternate resolution while the related complaint was held in abeyance; Western Law considered the student’s submissions only within the continuing-enrolment framework and issued a withdrawal decision. The record shows different offices addressing different parts of the same circumstances, but does not establish that any office made a coordinated determination of the full request for an academic completion pathway.

What remains to test against the policies: whether reprisal allegations received the distinct fact-finding treatment prescribed by MAPP 1.35 Procedures section V; whether the student was given an adequately explained and accessible process; whether accommodations and complaint-related impacts were individually considered; and whether an academic remedy was referred to a decision-maker with authority to consider it. These are questions raised by the documents, not findings of misconduct.

Subsequent events: 2 May decision → 10 June Ombudsman submission → 2026 LOP, enrolment and external proceedings.

Policy versions: The linked MAPP 1.35 Policy and Procedures both state an effective date of 6 February 2025. The linked Western Law academic policy describes the Continuing Enrolment framework, but the March 7 letter and May 2 decision are the primary evidence of the provisions applied in this case. No private email, personal address or medical document is published here.

July 9–11, 2025
Documented correspondence and outcome

Answers to earlier completion requests came after withdrawal

During March and April 2025, before I was required to withdraw, I had asked Western for a neutral decision-maker to address the grading-manipulation and academic-integrity concerns I had reported, and for a safe way to complete the JD. If Western could provide neither, I asked whether the degree could be conferred so that I could proceed to articling.

The May 2 decision required my withdrawal from the Faculty of Law without resolving those requested safeguards or providing a completion route. More than two months later, after my July 9 meeting with the newly appointed dean, his July 11 written follow-up finally answered the three routes I had raised:

Degree conferral so I could proceed to articling: The response stated that the University Senate governs conferral, that my academic requirements had not been met and that this option was not available.

Return to Western: The response identified an appeal to the Senate Review Board Academic (SRBA), said it may fall outside the prescribed time limits, and referred me to the University Ombudsperson for advice on whether and how an appeal remained possible. It did not provide the neutral decision-maker or safe academic conditions I had requested.

Completion at another law school: The response described a Letter of Permission (LOP) route, available only to students in good standing, with process information to follow.

The sequence matters: I sought answers and safeguards before withdrawal, but the written answer came only afterward. These were responses to requested routes, not three approved offers of degree completion. The subsequent records show what happened when I attempted to pursue them.

Evidence: March–April 2025 requests; May 2, 2025 withdrawal decision; July 9 meeting arrangements; July 11, 2025 written follow-up.
