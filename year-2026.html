What the repeated completion routes actually cost

The continuing issue was not unwillingness to complete the JD. The student sought a neutral academic decision-maker, separation from officials identified in her human-rights complaints, workable accommodations including Zoom participation when travel was unaffordable, and a viable means of completing outstanding requirements. She maintains that unsubmitted or unassessed work, affected by unresolved accommodations and neutral-grading concerns, contributed to the recorded F grades. Those grades have consequences for future admissions, but the transcript alone does not explain the circumstances of missing assessments or establish the quality of work that was never graded. These are contested academic facts requiring the underlying assessments and accommodation record.

The successive doors: before May 2, 2025, the request was for independent academic resolution and a completion path, not a formal written continuing-enrolment petition. The later dean’s July 2025 response described degree conferral as unavailable, SRBA as an appeal subject to time limits, and LOP as conditional on good standing. In 2026, receiving schools required a Western-issued LOP, transcripts and references; Western maintained the good-standing condition. Osgoode closed the application after outstanding documents and deadlines, and U of T declined LOP admission without an individualized reason. A suggested route is not a usable route when its prerequisite is withheld or unresolved. July 2025 advice ↗ Western’s 2026 condition ↗ Osgoode’s outcome ↗.

Interim relief and renewed hope: the July 2026 Form 16 asked for urgent relief; it was denied without deciding the merits. The student reports that changes in the availability of previously implicated faculty created a potential window for a return under different academic leadership. Whether those absences removed every conflict is not independently established by the email record. Attempts at a Fall 2026 return continued through August and September, but no agreed academic completion route emerged. A 11 September administrative notice identified 24 September as the date for a student-status/access change, prompting a renewed Form 16 on 23 September.

Articling and career impact: a supportive articling employer preserved the opportunity through a further deferral to the 2027–28 period, but JD completion and licensing remained prerequisites. This is a significant delay to the intended start of professional practice and income; a deferred salary is not automatically a legally proven loss. The student has continued exploring other law schools and work while seeking to complete the degree, not abandoning it.

Support and practical costs in 2026

Professional support that I value: The articling employer approved an additional deferral and confirmed that the position would remain available for the 2027–28 period, provided I met the normal eligibility conditions. I am grateful that the opportunity was preserved while I was seeking a path to complete my JD. No employer, office location or staff member is identified in this public account.

Cost of trying available academic routes: Applying for transfer or visiting-student options required researching differing rules and deadlines, ordering transcripts, paying application fees where applicable, seeking a Letter of Permission and following up with university departments. Travel from Milton to London for meetings and attempts to resolve academic issues created additional costs and time burdens. The financial impact includes both out-of-pocket costs and the disruption to professional plans; figures remain unquantified until matched against source records.

What the evidence illustrates: Some institutions and individuals responded constructively, including the articling employer and staff who explained requirements. The unresolved questions concerned Western's authority to permit continued study or an external completion route and the accommodations needed to pursue it. This chronology is about institutional responsibility and viable remedies, not criticizing the people who offered assistance.
